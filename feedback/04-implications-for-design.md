# Implications for the design of AI regulatory sandboxes

This document outlines design-related implications that emerge from the identified gaps and risks. The observations are intended to support reflection on how AI regulatory sandboxes can effectively fulfil their role as learning and transition instruments within the framework of the AI Act.

The implications are descriptive in nature and do not constitute prescriptive recommendations.

---

## Balance between flexibility and comparability

Flexibility is a core characteristic of AI regulatory sandboxes, enabling adaptation to sectoral, organisational, and technological contexts. At the same time, excessive divergence between sandbox implementations may limit interoperability, comparability and the accumulation of regulatory learning.

From a design perspective, this suggests a potential role for shared reference points that preserve flexibility while enabling meaningful comparison across sandbox instances.

---

## Interoperability as a structural design consideration

Interoperability extends beyond technical compatibility and includes organisational, procedural, and governance dimensions.

Considering interoperability early in sandbox design can support the transferability of insights and reduce friction when sandbox outcomes inform broader regulatory or deployment contexts.

From a design perspective, interoperability is closely linked to shared security and governance assumptions. A common security-by-design baseline can support interoperability by establishing consistent expectations for system protection, risk management, and the handling of residual risks across sandbox environments, without prescribing specific technologies.

Similarly, interoperability over time depends on the ability to trace and compare evolving sandbox configurations. Design approaches that support versioning and auditability enable sandbox instances to remain comparable as assumptions, rules, and technical setups change, thereby strengthening interoperability not only between parallel environments but also across successive implementation cycles.

---

## Production relevance and transition pathways

The relationship between sandbox environments and later production settings influences the practical value of sandbox outcomes.

Design approaches that consider production relevance from the outset can help reduce uncertainty when transitioning from experimentation to deployment, particularly in regulated or high-impact domains.

---

## Data governance and trust

Clear data governance contributes to trust among participants and supports compliance with existing legal frameworks.

From a design perspective, making data-related assumptions, boundaries, and responsibilities explicit can enhance transparency and reduce ambiguity during sandbox operation.

---

## Versioning, traceability, and institutional memory

AI regulatory sandboxes operate over time and across multiple participation cycles, further technology development and release iteration.

Design approaches that support versioning and traceability can strengthen institutional memory, enable auditability, and allow regulatory insights to be revisited and contextualised in future initiatives.

---

## Integration of identity and access management

Identity and access management mechanisms play a role in balancing openness, accountability, and protection of sensitive processes.

In addition to human participants, technical services and automated components may also require identifiable and accountable identities within sandbox environments. Service identities can support secure interactions between systems, controlled access to resources, and traceable execution of automated processes.

Design considerations may include how identity-based access concepts for both human and technical actors align with existing European trust infrastructures, supporting transparency and responsibility without excluding participation.

---

## Summary observation

Taken together, these implications highlight the importance of viewing AI regulatory sandboxes as socio-technical systems whose design influences their effectiveness as platforms for learning, coordination, and transition under the AI Act.
