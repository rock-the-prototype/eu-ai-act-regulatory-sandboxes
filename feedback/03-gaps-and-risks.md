# Gaps and risks

This document outlines structural gaps and associated risks that may arise in the design and operation of AI regulatory sandboxes if certain operational aspects remain implicit or are left entirely to european national implementation choices.

The observations are derived from the alignment analysis with the draft implementing act and are presented in a descriptive manner.

---

## Fragmentation across sandbox implementations

In the absence of shared operational reference points, AI regulatory sandboxes may evolve into heterogeneous environments with differing assumptions, configurations, and maturity levels.

This fragmentation can limit interoperability between sandbox instances and reduce the ability to compare outcomes or transfer insights across sectors, jurisdictions, or implementation cycles.

---

## Reduced effectiveness of regulatory learning

AI regulatory sandboxes are intended to function as learning instruments. Where sandbox environments differ substantially in structure or operational depth, regulatory learning risks remaining local rather than cumulative.

Insights gained in one sandbox may not be reusable in another context, limiting their contribution to broader regulatory understanding and guidance development.

---

## Increased operational and security overhead

Operating multiple sandbox environments with divergent configurations can increase the effort required to ensure robustness, security, and compliance.

Security-relevant issues, architectural weaknesses, or governance challenges may need to be identified and addressed repeatedly across sandbox instances, rather than being mitigated within a shared or comparable reference environment.

---

## Unclear transition paths to production environments

Where the relationship between sandbox configurations and later production environments remains implicit, organisations may face uncertainty when transitioning from experimentation to deployment.

This uncertainty can affect architectural decisions, investment planning, and risk assessments, particularly in regulated or high-impact domains.

---

## Limited traceability and auditability over time

If versioning and auditability of sandbox configurations, assumptions, and rule sets are not explicitly addressed, it may become difficult to trace how regulatory interpretations and implementation practices evolve over time.

This can limit transparency towards oversight bodies and reduce the long-term value of sandbox initiatives as reference points for future regulatory or policy decisions.

---

## Summary observation

Taken together, these gaps do not indicate deficiencies in the intent of AI regulatory sandboxes. Rather, they highlight areas where implicit assumptions and decentralised implementation choices may introduce systemic risks that affect interoperability, efficiency, and long-term regulatory learning.
