# Requirements as structured, machine-readable artefacts

The directory [`/requirements`](./requirements) contains a concrete example of how selected requirements can be expressed as versioned, machine-readable artefacts.

The purpose of this directory is not to introduce new regulatory obligations, but to illustrate how design-related considerations and common requirements can be translated into concrete, auditable artefacts that support comparability, traceability, and reuse.

---

## Methodological background

The requirements in this directory are expressed using a structured approach inspired by the principles of the [**specification standard `dsl-core`**](https://github.com/rock-the-prototype/dsl-core).

This approach treats requirements as:
- explicit statements rather than implicit assumptions,
- versioned artefacts rather than static documentation,
- and verifiable objects that can be reviewed, compared, and evolved over time.

Such representations support regulatory learning by making assumptions, constraints, and evaluation criteria transparent and inspectable.

---

## Scope of machine readable requirement specification

The requirements focus on structural and operational aspects relevant to AI regulatory sandboxes, including:
- interoperability considerations,
- production relevance,
- data governance,
- security-by-design baselines,
- versioning and auditability,
- and identity and access management.

They are intended as a **prototypical demonstrative artefact** and do not constitute normative or legal requirements.

---

## Purpose within this repository

Within the context of this repository, the requirements artefacts serve as:
- a concrete extension of the analytical findings,
- a bridge between regulatory intent and operational design,
- and a reference for how iterative, versioned feedback processes can be supported by structured representations.

They illustrate how regulatory feedback and design implications can move beyond narrative text and be made directly usable in technical and organisational contexts.
